# RD-5: standards prices, previews, terms and the J1979-2 dates (Q9)

Written 2026-11-24 (sprint day 2). Reading only. Not legal advice. Clean-room rule held: only public store pages, public regulator text and public terms pages were read. No standard text was copied; no leaked or pirated copy was opened or linked.

Status: PARTIAL. ISO, ANSI and DIN Media pages loaded and are the basis for every price. The SAE own store pages (sae.org, saemobilus.sae.org) did not show prices, so every SAE price below is a reseller price. The ISO Online Browsing Platform preview was not opened (it needs a click on "I agree to the Terms of Use", which is the owner's call). The eCFR and Federal Register own sites were not read; US federal text came from Cornell LII and govinfo.

## ANSWER FIRST

1. **ISO prices are confirmed from iso.org** (CHF, PDF + ePub, English). ISO 14229-1:2026 is CHF 227. ISO 15765-2:2024 is CHF 181. ISO 13400-2:2025 is CHF 204. Pair that matters most (14229-1 plus 15765-2) is CHF 408, about USD 450 at the plan's own estimated rate (rate not re-checked today).
2. **The plan was citing withdrawn editions.** ISO 14229-1:2020 (CHF 221 in `resources.md`) is withdrawn and replaced by the 2026 edition (June 2026, 427 pages). ISO 13400-2:2019 (CHF 199) is withdrawn too. `resources.md` section 5.1 needs the new editions and prices.
3. **J1979-2 is already required by California for model year 2027 and later, and it is optional for 2023 to 2026.** This comes from the CARB final regulation order text (13 CCR 1968.2), read directly. So "2027" is now confirmed for California. The 2027 date is not a single-source claim any more.
4. **The federal (EPA) date is moving.** The federal light-duty OBD section (40 CFR 86.1806-27) says model year 2027, but EPA proposed on 2026-05-18 to push it to 2029. It is a proposal. I found no final rule. California's own date was not changed by anything I read.
5. **SAE prices (resellers only):** J1979 about USD 222 (ANSI) or EUR 206.50 (DIN Media); J1979-2 USD 216 (ANSI) or EUR 201.20; J2012 USD 151 or EUR 140.20; J2012-DA (the DTC text spreadsheet) EUR 963 incl. VAT (EUR 900 ex VAT). SAE own price: unconfirmed.
6. **ASAM and AUTOSAR need no purchase for us.** ASAM standards are member-free, non-member price is on request only, and the licence limits passing them on. AUTOSAR files are free to download for information only; commercial use needs a partnership.
7. **Recommendation: buy nothing today.** One conditional buy: ISO 14229-1:2026 (CHF 227) only if a named task below stays blocked after captures A and B. Details and the named tasks are in section 7.
8. Every price is a list price seen on 2026-11-24 and can change. Currency conversion is an estimate and unconfirmed.

Why the SAE docs matter in plain words:

- J1979 is the list of classic OBD-II modes and PIDs. Free public tables already cover most of it.

## 8. What a purchase does not change (licence reminders)

- Buying a standard gives the buyer a licence to read it. The ISO and SAE copies are for the purchaser. They must not be copied into the repo or the public notes, and tables or text cannot be pasted into our docs. ASAM spells this out. AUTOSAR says information-only use.
- Standards are paywalled but their ideas are used widely through open code. The repo's rule stays: implement from public summaries, captures and open-source oracles; use a paid standard only to settle a dispute and cite by number, not by text.
- Not legal advice. Whether implementing a protocol described in a paid standard needs any licence is a question for counsel; it is not answered by any page I read.
